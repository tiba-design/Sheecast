Sheep castration guide: closed castration with the Burdizzo

A bloodless technique: the elements of the spermatic cord are crushed through intact skin, so the testis atrophies without any open wound. A study guide for Special Surgery before hands-on training.

  • No skin wound
  • Lower risk of fly strike and infection
  • Breeding and aggression control
  • Improved meat quality

  5. 5

  6. 6
    Steps 4 and 5Burdizzo bloodless emasculatomeMust be clean, in good condition, with aligned jaws. Its jaws crush the isolated spermatic cord without cutting the skin and are held closed for roughly 10 to 12 seconds.
  7. 7
    Step 6Burdizzo again second cordRepeated on the opposite cord, at a different level, then the animal is monitored.

Comparison and complications

The choice of method depends on age, facilities and the animal’s welfare.

MethodWoundAdvantageNote
Burdizzo (closed)NonePractically bloodlessFails if the cord is not fully crushed
Elastrator ringNone, then necrosis and sloughingFast and simpleYoung lambs; longer pain and higher tetanus risk
Open surgical orchiectomyScrotal incisionReliable at any ageNeeds anesthesia, asepsis and wound care
  • Incomplete castration: the cord was not crushed properly, so re-examine the scrotum after a few weeks.
  • Scrotal skin necrosis: from crushing skin too hard, or crushing both cords at the same level.
  • Transient scrotal swelling, which needs monitoring.
  • Tetanus, pain and stress: plan prophylaxis, analgesia and calm handling.

Aftercare and follow-up

Closed castration still needs monitoring. Use this timeline for the flock record.

  1. First 24 to 48 hoursKeep animals in a clean, dry area. Mild scrotal swelling is expected. Check appetite, gait and any straining.
  2. First weekLook for heat, severe pain, discharge or flies at the scrotum. Call the veterinarian if swelling keeps increasing. Watch for stiffness as an early sign of tetanus.
  3. Weeks 4 to 6Re-examine: the testes should be shrinking and firm, and the cord should not be palpable. A normal-sized testis means failed castration and needs repeating.
  4. RecordsNote date, age, operator, any complications and the result of the re-examination.

For educators and clinicians

Teaching and assessment aids for veterinary programmes, clinical instruction and continuing education.

Learning outcomes

  • Describe the layers of the scrotum and the contents of the spermatic cord in the ram.
  • Justify the choice between Burdizzo, banding and surgical castration for a given animal, age and setting.
  • Sequence the closed-castration steps, including cord isolation and the level of the crush.
  • Recognise incomplete castration and other complications, and plan follow-up.
  • Apply welfare principles: analgesia, handling and local legal limits.

Discussion prompts

  • Histology showed incomplete involution in most lambs after Burdizzo castration (Ref 1). What does this mean for owner counselling and follow-up?
  • Which factors could affect crush completeness: age, cord thickness, level, hold time, instrument condition?
  • Compare Burdizzo and banding for pain, complications and failure (Refs 2, 5, 7).
  • When is surgical castration the better choice?

Welfare and regulation

  • Age limits, who may perform castration, and analgesia rules differ by country and change over time; check local law. For example, Irish guidance limits non-veterinary Burdizzo use in cattle by age (Ref 6).
  • Local anesthesia and analgesics are discussed in the welfare literature as ways to reduce pain (Ref 7).
  • One source regards the emasculatome as a very inhumane method (Ref 5), so justify the method and record the decision.

Evidence gaps

  • Most quantitative data come from cattle; sheep-specific histology is limited.
  • The ideal crush level, hold time and age for lambs are not firmly established in the sources found.
  • The outcome timeline in this guide is illustrative; replace it with your own data where available.
